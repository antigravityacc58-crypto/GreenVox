# GreenVox Social Movement + Environmental Use Guide

## Movement proposition

**Map. Act. Grow.**

GreenVox should not be presented as a website that “tells people where to plant trees.”
It is a community evidence loop:

**Satellite evidence → public understanding → field verification → community discussion → local action → monitoring again.**

## Why the school matters

The school is the movement anchor rather than the only study object.
Students can introduce environmental monitoring to the surrounding community while the 250 m pilot area remains small enough to verify on foot.

## Mission 01 — Verify Vegetation Change

### Goal
Collect at least 50 anonymous ground observations in the 250 m pilot area.

### Participants
- students;
- teachers / project mentors;
- nearby residents invited through a QR code;
- environmental/community volunteers.

### What participants do
1. Open the public GreenVox link.
2. Choose **Pilot Area**.
3. Read the satellite evidence.
4. Activate **Survey Mode**.
5. Visit or visually verify a real location.
6. Classify it as:
   - Tree / Canopy
   - Grass / Low Vegetation
   - Concrete / Asphalt / Hardscape
   - Open / Exposed Area
7. Add a short environmental note.
8. Save the observation.

## What happens after observations are collected

The movement should not jump immediately to planting.

A school/community review can ask:
- Which low-signal areas are actually hard surfaces?
- Which are functional roads, sports areas, or buildings and therefore not planting targets?
- Which sites already contain vegetation that the satellite pixel does not fully represent?
- Which open/exposed areas are realistically suitable for shade, planting, protection, or maintenance?

## Environmental outputs

Measurable outputs can include:
- historical mean NDVI;
- percentage of valid area in decrease/stable/increase screening classes;
- number and location of field observations;
- proportion of field observations classified as vegetation vs hard/exposed surfaces;
- repeat satellite monitoring after a later comparable seasonal period.

## Social movement outputs

Measurable participation outputs can include:
- number of anonymous observations;
- number of participating classes or community groups (tracked offline by the project team, not through personal web data);
- number of verified priority sites;
- number of community discussions or actions arising from verified evidence;
- repeat participation in later monitoring rounds.

## Booth demonstration

A strong 3-minute demonstration:

1. **Problem:** “Satellite data suggests the community vegetation signal is lower than in 2022.”
2. **Evidence:** show the 2022 vs 2026 metrics.
3. **Map:** switch Jakarta View → Pilot Area.
4. **Participation:** activate Survey Mode and submit one sample field observation.
5. **Impact:** show the community counter update.
6. **Message:** “GreenVox does not turn a satellite pixel directly into a planting instruction. It turns it into a question that students and the community verify together.”
